How a keystroke happens

  1. Capture. OpenCV reads the webcam at 30–60 fps and mirrors it so your hand moves the way you expect.
  2. Track. MediaPipe Hands returns 21 landmarks per hand. Landmark 8 is the index tip, 4 is the thumb tip.
  3. Smooth. A One Euro filter on the tip removes jitter when you hold still but stays responsive on fast moves.
  4. Hit-test. The smoothed tip maps into keyboard space. The nearest key within a 15% snap radius gets the hover state.
  5. Detect press. Pinch distance (4↔8) divided by palm width (0↔9). Press below 0.22, release above 0.35, so one pinch is one key.
  6. Emit. Send the character to the app's text box, or to the OS with pynput so it types into any window.

Design rules

Why it looks like this

  • Keys are at least 6% of frame width. Smaller targets fail with webcam jitter.
  • The keyboard sits low and tilts back, so your arm rests near your desk instead of floating at shoulder height.
  • Translucent keys keep your hand visible through them. You need to see your finger to aim.
  • Three-stage feedback: glow on hover, key sinks on press, a short click sound on emit.
  • Word suggestions above the keyboard cut the number of pinches roughly in half.
  • The HUD shows tracking confidence. When it drops under 0.6 the cursor greys out instead of typing garbage.

Build it

Suggested stack

  • Python desktop: opencv-python + mediapipe for tracking, pynput for system typing. Draw the keyboard with OpenCV or a pygame overlay.
  • Browser version: MediaPipe Tasks HandLandmarker in JS, <video> + canvas overlay. Zero install, easy to share.
  • Milestone 1: fingertip cursor and hover. 2: pinch with hysteresis. 3: smoothing and snap. 4: suggestions and gestures.

Expect 15–25 wpm once tuned. Physical keyboards sit around 40.
